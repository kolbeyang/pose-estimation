## Result

## Experiments Conducted

### Experiment 1: Rotation Penalty Scalar Sweep
- **Hypothesis**: Rotation penalties too weak, heatmap gradient overwhelms smoothing
- **Result**: Negative. Scalar 10→500 had negligible effect on VW-SI-MPJPE. Not the bottleneck.

### Experiment 2: Num Steps Sweep
- **Hypothesis**: Too many optimization steps distort MotionBert's already-smooth output
- **Result**: Positive. Steps=5 → +2.3%, Steps=10 → +1.5%, Steps=50 → -6.6%. Monotonic improvement with fewer steps.
- **Fix applied**: `num_steps=5` in `run_motionbert/run_full_config.json` and `run_single_config.json`. MediaPipe retains `num_steps=50`.

### Experiment 3: SI-MPJPE Metric Consistency
- **Hypothesis**: Scale factor optimization might differ between metric calculation and optimizer
- **Result**: All 12 consistency tests pass. No bug found. Scale optimization is mathematically correct.

### Experiment 4: Visibility Weighting Investigation (SPEC Hypothesis 3)
- **Hypothesis**: VW logic might incorrectly handle MotionBert confidence values
- **Result**: No bug. VW uses binary in-frame check on GT projections. Invisible joints have 5.8x higher error — VW correctly excludes them. Ankles rarely visible (0.3-0.9%), upper body nearly always (93-100%).

### Experiment 5: Per-Joint Speed Analysis (SPEC Hypothesis 1)
- **Hypothesis**: Optimizer over-smooths elbows/wrists/knees
- **Result**: The original hypothesis was wrong. Steps=50 doesn't dampen speeds — it **over-corrects** them to 1.21x GT speed (introduces jitter). Steps=5 preserves detector-level speeds at 0.81x GT. MotionBert's detector itself under-estimates arm speeds (0.68-0.72x GT) because it produces temporally smooth output.

---

## Key Insight

MotionBert and MediaPipe need fundamentally different optimization strategies:
- **MediaPipe** (noisy input, high MPJVE ~3-7 cm/f): Benefits from aggressive optimization (50 steps). Heatmap gradient adds useful signal to noisy poses.
- **MotionBert** (smooth input, low MPJVE ~0.2-3 cm/f): Just 5 steps of gentle refinement. More steps = heatmap gradient introduces oscillations into already-smooth output.
