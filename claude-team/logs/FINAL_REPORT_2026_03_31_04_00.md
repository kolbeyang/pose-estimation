# Final Report: Optimizer Tuning — MotionBert & MediaPipe

**Date:** 2026-03-31
**Scope:** Hyperparameter tuning to make the FK optimizer improve (not hurt) both pipelines

---

## Starting Point

When this work began, the optimizer was **actively hurting MotionBert**:

| Pipeline | VW-SI-MPJPE Change | Examples Improved |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| MotionBert | **-6.6%** (worse) | 6/25 |
| MediaPipe | +2.1% | 11/25 |

Both pipelines used identical optimization settings: `num_steps=50`, `learning_rate=0.002`, `heatmap_blur_sigma=4.0`.

## Final State

| Pipeline | VW-SI-MPJPE Change | Examples Improved |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| MotionBert | **+3.06%** | **19/25** |
| MediaPipe | **+2.91%** | **17/25** |

That's a **+9.7 percentage point swing** for MotionBert (from -6.6% to +3.1%).

---

## Root Cause Analysis

### Why did the optimizer hurt MotionBert?

MotionBert's detector output is already temporally smooth (MPJVE ~1.8 cm/frame vs MediaPipe's ~4.5 cm/frame). The optimizer was running 50 Adam steps at LR=0.002 with sharp heatmaps (blur=4), which meant:

1. **Each frame's heatmap gradient independently pulled joints** toward the SH heatmap peaks
2. Since heatmap peaks are noisy frame-to-frame, this **introduced oscillations** into already-smooth trajectories
3. The smoothing penalties (rotation_penalty_scalar=10) were too weak to counteract this at 50 steps

Evidence: On the worst-regressing example (`171204_pose3_2000`), the optimizer increased mean joint speed from 0.16 cm/frame (close to GT's 0.13) to 0.25 cm/frame (1.9x GT). Individual joints like LElbow went from 0.98x GT speed to 3.14x GT speed.

### Why does the optimizer help MediaPipe?

MediaPipe's detector output is noisy — high frame-to-frame jitter. The heatmap gradient provides useful signal that pulls joints toward correct positions. More optimization steps = more correction of noisy input.

### Key insight

**The two pipelines need fundamentally different optimization strategies.** MotionBert needs gentle refinement (few steps, low LR, blurred heatmaps). MediaPipe needs aggressive correction (many steps, moderate LR, sharp heatmaps).

## Experiments

### Experiment 1: num_steps reduction (the breakthrough)

| Observation | MotionBert VW-SI-MPJPE gets worse with 50 steps. Velocity error increases on 19/25 examples. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Fewer steps = less time for heatmap gradient to introduce oscillations. |
| **Verification** | Swept num_steps in [5, 10, 20, 30, 50] on 5 representative examples. |
| **Result** | Monotonically better with fewer steps. Steps=5 → +2.3%, steps=10 → +1.5%, steps=50 → -6.6%. |
| **Solution** | Set `num_steps=5` for MotionBert (later refined to 10 with lower LR). |

### Experiment 2: Learning rate + heatmap blur (round 1 refinement)

| Observation | Even at steps=5, wrists/elbows consistently hurt (-0.31 cm). Examples with already-low error tend to get worse. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | LR=0.002 is too aggressive for MotionBert's clean data. Higher blur smooths noisy SH heatmap gradients. |
| **Verification** | Swept LR ∈ {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.004} × blur ∈ {2, 4, 8, 12} on 5 examples. |
| **Result** | LR=0.0005 best. Blur=12 best. Combined: LR=0.0005 + blur=12 improved 3/5 → was 2/5. |
| **Solution** | MotionBert: `learning_rate=0.0005`, `heatmap_blur_sigma=12.0`. |
| **Full validation** | +2.6% VW-SI-MPJPE (19/25 improved). Flipped 6 examples from worse to better. |

### Experiment 3: MediaPipe LR tuning

| Observation | MediaPipe only improves 11/25 on VW-SI-MPJPE despite 14.8% velocity improvement. Some examples overshoot. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Lower LR reduces position overshoot while retaining velocity smoothing. |
| **Verification** | Swept LR ∈ {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002} × steps ∈ {10, 20, 50, 80} on 5 examples. |
| **Result** | LR=0.001 + steps=50 is the sweet spot. |
| **Solution** | MediaPipe: `learning_rate=0.001`. |
| **Full validation** | +2.9% VW-SI-MPJPE (17/25 improved), up from +2.1% (11/25). |

### Experiment 4: Rotation penalty multipliers (negative result)

| Observation | Wrists/elbows are the most-hurt joints. They have the lowest rotation penalties (0.1, 0.3). |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Higher wrist/elbow penalties would dampen arm jitter. |
| **Verification** | Tested 3 penalty variants on 5 examples. |
| **Result** | All within 0.002 cm of each other. **No effect.** At LR=0.0005, rotations barely move — penalties are irrelevant. |
| **Decision** | **Reverted.** |

### Experiment 5: Fine-tuning steps + blur (round 2)

| Observation | 6 MotionBert examples still get worse. Ankle joints are the main culprit (RAnkle +3.9 cm in worse group). |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | More blur smooths noisy ankle heatmaps. At LR=0.0005, more steps should now be safe. |
| **Verification** | Swept steps ∈ {5, 7, 10} × blur ∈ {12, 16, 20, 24} on 10 examples. |
| **Result** | steps=10 + blur=16 is optimal. More steps monotonically helps at this low LR. |
| **Solution** | MotionBert: `num_steps=10`, `heatmap_blur_sigma=16.0`. |
| **Full validation** | +3.06% VW-SI-MPJPE (19/25). |

### Experiment 6: SI-MPJPE metric consistency (verification)

| Observation | SPEC noted that SI metrics shouldn't degrade if scale=1.0 is in the search space. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Possible mismatch between scale optimization and metric calculation. |
| **Verification** | Wrote `test_si_consistency.py` with 4 checks: (a) SI scale minimizes MPJPE, (b) VW-SI consistent with VW-MPJPE after scaling, (c) scale=1.0 ≥ optimal, (d) optimization doesn't break SI consistency. |
| **Result** | All 12 tests pass (4 checks × 3 examples). **No bug.** Scale optimization is mathematically correct. |

### Experiment 7: Visibility weighting investigation

| Observation | SPEC asked about potential VW logic issues. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | VW might incorrectly handle MotionBert confidence values. |
| **Verification** | Analyzed visibility weight distributions. Checked invisible joint error rates. Compared VW-MPJPE vs MPJPE optimization direction agreement. |
| **Result** | **No bug.** VW uses binary in-frame check on GT projections. Invisible joints have 5.8x higher error — VW correctly excludes them. Ankles rarely visible (0.3-0.9%), upper body nearly always (93-100%). VW and raw MPJPE agree on optimization direction 80% of the time. |

### Experiment 8: Joint speed analysis (SPEC Hypothesis 1 verification)

| Observation | SPEC asked to verify over-smoothing by comparing joint speeds. |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | Optimizer over-smooths elbows/wrists/knees. |
| **Verification** | Computed average per-joint speed for GT, detector, old-optimized (steps=50), and new-optimized (steps=10) across all 25 examples. |
| **Result** | The original hypothesis was **wrong**. Steps=50 doesn't dampen speeds below GT — it **over-corrects** them to 1.21x GT speed (introducing jitter). Steps=10 preserves detector-level speeds at 0.81x GT. MotionBert's detector itself under-estimates arm speeds (0.68-0.72x GT) because it produces temporally smooth output. |

## What Didn't Work

| Thing Tried | Why It Failed |
|---|---|
| Rotation penalty scalar (10→500) | At LR=0.0005, rotations barely move. Penalty is irrelevant. |
| Rotation penalty multipliers (higher wrist/elbow) | Same reason — no effect at low LR. |
| Position penalty weight (500→5000) | Only affects root inter-frame smoothness. Not the bottleneck. |

---

## Remaining Limitations

1. **6 MotionBert examples still get worse.** Root cause: ankle heatmaps are noisy in these sequences (ankles rarely visible, 0.3-0.9% of frames). The optimizer follows bad heatmap signal for ankles, increasing RAnkle error by +3.9 cm in the worst group. This is a detector limitation, not an optimizer limitation.

2. **MotionBert absolute MPJPE is high (24 cm)** due to depth/scale estimation errors. VW-SI-MPJPE (12 cm) is more meaningful after excluding invisible joints and correcting scale.

3. **MediaPipe velocity improvement traded off slightly** when LR was reduced (14.8% → 10.1% MPJVE improvement). Position accuracy gained more than velocity lost.
