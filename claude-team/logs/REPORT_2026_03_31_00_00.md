## Experiment 1: MotionBert Learning Rate + Blur Sigma

### Observation
- MotionBert already has smooth trajectories (low velocity error baseline: 1.84 cm)
- Optimizer adds jitter from noisy Stacked Hourglass heatmap gradients
- 10/25 examples got worse with current config (lr=0.002, blur_sigma=4.0, num_steps=5)
- Wrists/elbows consistently hurt (-0.31cm each) while knees/hips improve
- Examples that got worse tended to already have low error (good detector output)

### Hypothesis
1. Learning rate is too high -- optimizer overshoots on clean MotionBert data
2. Heatmap blur sigma too low -- sharp SH heatmaps create noisy gradients
3. Combined: lower LR + higher blur should reduce jitter while preserving good updates

### Verification Method
Swept 4 LR values (0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.004), 4 blur sigmas (2, 4, 8, 12), and 7 combined configs on 5 representative examples (2 improved, 2 worse, 1 borderline).

### Verification Results
- LR sweep: LR=0.0005 best (-1.046cm mean delta vs -0.907cm baseline)
- Blur sweep: sigma=12 best (-1.062cm, improved 3/5 vs 2/5)
- Combined: lr=0.0005 + blur=12 gives -1.092cm, 3/5 improved
- Rotation penalty multiplier changes had negligible effect

### Solution
Changed MotionBert config: `learning_rate: 0.002 -> 0.0005`, `heatmap_blur_sigma: 4.0 -> 12.0`

### Solution Results (full 25 examples)
- VW-SI-MPJPE: 12.24 -> 11.92 cm (+2.6%, was +2.3%)
- Examples improved: 19/25 (was 15/25)
- Flipped 6 examples from worse to better (pose1_16000, pose1_22000, pose1_26000, pose2_2000, pose2_27000, pose3_2000)
- VW-SI-MPJVE: +7.4% (was +4.7%)

### Decision: KEEP

## Experiment 2: MediaPipe Learning Rate

### Observation
- MediaPipe only improves 11/25 examples on VW-SI-MPJPE despite strong 14.8% velocity improvement
- Some examples get much worse (pose1_16000: +2.47cm) while velocity improves greatly (-1.24cm)
- The optimizer overshoots on position while successfully smoothing trajectories

### Hypothesis
Lower learning rate will reduce position overshoot while retaining most velocity benefit.

### Verification Method
Swept LR (0.0005, 0.001, 0.002), steps (10, 20, 50, 80), and position_penalty_weight (500, 1000, 2000) on 5 representative examples.

### Verification Results
- lr=0.001 + steps=50: best simple change (-0.311cm vs -0.253cm baseline, 3/5 vs 2/5)
- lr=0.001 + steps=80: slightly better (-0.378cm) but slower runtime
- Higher position penalty (2000): worse than lower LR approach

### Solution
Changed MediaPipe config: `learning_rate: 0.002 -> 0.001`

### Solution Results (full 25 examples)
- VW-SI-MPJPE: 11.06 -> 10.74 cm (+2.9%, was +2.1%)
- Examples improved: 17/25 (was 11/25)
- VW-SI-MPJVE: +10.1% (was +14.8%) -- slight tradeoff

### Decision: KEEP

## Experiment 3: Rotation Penalty Multipliers (MotionBert)

### Observation
Wrists and elbows are the joints most hurt by optimization (-0.31cm wrists, -0.05cm elbows). These joints have low rotation penalty multipliers (0.1 for wrists, 0.3 for elbows), allowing more jitter.

### Hypothesis
Increasing wrist/elbow rotation penalties would dampen arm jitter.

### Verification Method
Tested 3 variants of rotation_penalty_multipliers (higher arm, much higher arm, uniform) on 5 examples with the best LR/blur config.

### Verification Results
All variants produced nearly identical results (within 0.002cm of each other). The rotation penalty has negligible effect compared to LR and blur changes.

### Decision: REVERT (no change needed)

# Round 2 — Developer Dan (2026-03-31 afternoon)

## Objective
Improve MotionBert optimization further, focusing on the 6 examples that still got worse after round 1.

## 1. Deep Observation: Why 6 Examples Get Worse

The 6 worse examples share a common pattern: **ankle joints move in the wrong direction** during optimization.

Per-joint error analysis (averaged over groups):
- **WORSE group (6 examples):** Ankles get significantly worse (RAnkle +3.90 cm, LAnkle +2.24 cm), wrists also degrade (RWrist +1.45 cm)
- **BETTER group (19 examples):** All joints improve, especially ankles (RAnkle -1.65 cm) and knees (LKnee -1.67 cm)

Ankles have the highest absolute error (~80-100 cm) in both groups. The optimizer relies on heatmap signal which is unreliable for ankle joints in these specific sequences.

## 2. Experiments

### Experiment 2A: num_steps fine sweep (2-7)
- **Hypothesis:** Fewer steps might reduce harm to worse group.
- **Result:** More steps is monotonically better. At LR=0.0005, even 7 steps is conservative.
- Steps 7 gives -0.583cm delta vs steps 5 at -0.569cm.

### Experiment 2B: position_penalty_weight (500, 1000, 2000, 5000)
- **Hypothesis:** Higher penalty constrains optimizer.
- **Result:** No effect. Penalty only affects root inter-frame smoothness, irrelevant at this LR.

### Experiment 2C: heatmap_blur_sigma (12, 16, 20, 24)
- **Hypothesis:** More blur smooths noisy heatmap peaks.
- **Result:** blur=16 is optimal (-0.585 vs -0.569 for blur=12). Consistent improvement.

### Experiment 2D: Ankle/wrist rotation penalty multipliers
- **Result:** No effect at LR=0.0005 / 5 steps. Optimizer barely moves rotations.

### Experiment 2E: Combined steps x blur (final sweep)
- Best config: **num_steps=10, heatmap_blur_sigma=16.0** (-0.632 delta, best overall + best velocity)
- Improvement is monotonic: more steps + more blur = better.
